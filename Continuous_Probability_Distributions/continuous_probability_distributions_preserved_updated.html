Mathematical Formulas

What does the z-score do?

z = (x − μ) / σ

The z-score converts a measurement into the number of standard deviations it is from the mean. A positive z-score is above the mean, a negative z-score is below it, and zero is exactly at the mean. Standardizing values lets us use the standard Normal CDF to calculate probabilities.

Mathematical Formulas

Formulas & the memoryless property

f(t) = λe−λt, t ≥ 0
P(T ≤ t) = 1 − e−λt
P(T > t) = e−λt
P(T > t₂ | T > t₁) = e−λ(t₂−t₁)

Memoryless property in simple language: If no request has arrived yet, the time already spent waiting does not change the probability distribution of the additional wait. For example, after waiting 3 seconds with no request, the chance of waiting more than another 5 seconds is the same as the chance of waiting more than 5 seconds from the start. This property applies to the ideal Exponential model.

Model assumption: Requests are assumed to follow a constant-rate Poisson arrival process. Real systems may have changing traffic rates, bursts, or dependencies, so this is an illustrative model rather than a guarantee about actual traffic.

CLT Explanation

How does it work?

Imagine repeatedly selecting groups of n orders and calculating each group's average processing time. Those averages form a sampling distribution. As sample size increases, this distribution tends to become approximately Normal under suitable conditions, even when individual processing times are not Normally distributed. This assumes independent observations and a population with finite variance.

Important: The CLT describes sample averages, not individual processing times. The displayed probabilities use a Normal approximation for the sample mean.
